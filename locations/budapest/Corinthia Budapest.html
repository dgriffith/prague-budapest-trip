Corinthia Budapest

Our hotel for the Budapest stay, situated on Erzsébet körút (Elizabeth Boulevard) — the Grand Boulevard that sweeps through Districts VI and VII.

Details

  • Address: Erzsébet körút 43-49, Budapest 1073 (District VII)
  • Neighbourhood: Erzsébetváros (Elizabeth Town) / former Jewish Quarter
  • Chain: Corinthia Hotels International
  • Website: corinthia.com/budapest
  • Rooms: 414 rooms, 31 suites
  • Originally opened: 1896 (as the Grand Hotel Royal); restored 2003

Location Notes

The hotel is said to be part of the inspiration for Wes Anderson’s The Grand Budapest Hotel. Situated on the Grand Boulevard, it’s within easy reach of:

  • Oktogon (junction of Andrássy út and the Grand Boulevard) — ~5 min walk
  • Andrássy Avenue (UNESCO-listed, lined with embassies and cafés) — right nearby
  • Heroes’ Square & City Park — ~20 min walk along Andrássy
  • Great Synagogue (Dohány utca) — ~10 min walk
  • Ruin bars (Szimpla Kert, etc.) — in the immediate neighbourhood
  • Blaha Lujza tér metro station (M2 Red line) — ~5 min walk

Nearby Restaurants

  • Stand25 Bistro — Bib Gourmand; refined traditional Hungarian, Buda side (~15 min)
  • Borkonyha Winekitchen — Michelin-starred wine-focused modern Hungarian (~20 min walk/short taxi)
  • Babel Budapest — Michelin-starred tasting menu, inner city (~20 min walk)
  • New York Café — grand café on the same boulevard (~5 min walk)
  • Szimpla Kert — the original ruin bar, in the neighbourhood (~10 min walk)
  • Menza — retro Hungarian bistro on Liszt Ferenc tér (~10 min walk)

Nearby Museums

Most Budapest museums are closed on Mondays, and the Dohány Street Synagogue is closed on Saturdays.

Walkable (10–15 min)

  • House of Terror — must-see; Arrow Cross and communist secret police HQ on Andrássy
  • Liszt Ferenc Memorial Museum — Liszt’s apartment, close to the House of Terror
  • Dohány Street Synagogue & Hungarian Jewish Museum — must-see; Europe’s largest synagogue and the Jewish Museum
  • Hungarian National Museum — national history, on Múzeum körút (~15–20 min)

City Park (M1 metro or ~25 min walk up Andrássy)

  • Museum of Fine Arts (Szépművészeti Múzeum) — must-see; Old Masters and Egyptian collection
  • Museum of Ethnography (Néprajzi Múzeum) — Hungarian folk culture, in the new Liget building
  • House of Music Hungary (Magyar Zene Háza) — interactive music museum by Sou Fujimoto

Across the Danube / further out (taxi or public transport)

  • Hungarian Parliament Building — must-see; book guided tours ahead
  • Hungarian National Gallery — Hungarian art, in the Royal Palace
  • Budapest History Museum (Castle Museum) — medieval palace remains, also in the Royal Palace
  • Hospital in the Rock (Sziklakórház) — WWII hospital and Cold War bunker under Castle Hill; guided tours only
  • Vasarely Museum — op art in Óbuda (~20 min)
  • Memento Park — communist statue park on the city’s edge; half-day trip

Baths

The Gellért Baths closed in October 2025 for renovation and aren’t due to reopen until 2028. The Király Baths have been closed since 2019 with no reopening date.

  • Széchenyi Thermal Bath — must-see; the grand neo-baroque bath in City Park (M1 metro or ~25 min walk)
  • Rudas Thermal Bath — must-see; 16th-century Ottoman bath with a rooftop pool and Fri–Sat night bathing (taxi, ~15 min)
  • Lukács Thermal Bath — the locals’ bath, quiet and cheaper (trams 4/6 from outside the hotel)
  • Veli Bej Bath — small, uncrowded Ottoman bath next to Lukács; afternoons only on weekdays
  • Dandár Thermal Bath — cheap, local art deco bath in District IX, Pest side

Nearby Shops

Walkable (5–15 min)

  • Massolit Books and Cafe — English-language bookshop and café in the Jewish Quarter (~5–10 min)
  • Printa — Hungarian design shop and screen-printing studio on Rumbach utca (~10 min)
  • Gombolyda — yarn shop down the Grand Boulevard past Blaha Lujza tér (~10 min)
  • Eventuell Gallery — handmade Hungarian textiles in the inner city; check it’s still open (~20 min)
  • Írók Boltja (Writers' Bookshop) — the literary bookshop on Andrássy út; English books upstairs (~5–10 min)
  • Retrock — the city’s best vintage clothing store, off Deák tér (~15 min)
  • Paloma Artspace — showroom for Hungarian designers near Astoria (~15–20 min, or one M2 stop)
  • Máthé Textil — big fabric-by-the-metre shop by Keleti station (~15 min, or one M2 stop)
  • Fűszerész — specialist spice shop with ~400 spices; good for paprika. Opposite the Dohány Street Synagogue (~10–15 min)
  • Szimpla Sunday Farmers' Market — Sundays only, inside Szimpla Kert; local producers and tasting platters (~10 min)

Short ride

  • 1001fonal — the city’s best-known yarn shop, near Margaret Bridge (trams 4/6 from outside the hotel)
  • Stageshop — boutique and vintage guitars with a luthier, Buda side (M2 metro to Batthyány tér)
  • Herend Porcelain (József nádor tér) — flagship shop of Hungary’s famous porcelain maker, inner city (~25 min walk)
  • Folkart Kézművesház — authentic Hungarian folk art: embroidery, pottery, folk clothing; off Váci utca (~25 min walk, or M2 to Deák tér)

Further out

  • Ecseri Flea Market (Ecseri Piac) — huge antiques and flea market in District XIX; half-day trip, bring cash
