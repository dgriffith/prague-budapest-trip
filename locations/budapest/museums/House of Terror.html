House of Terror

  • Address: Andrássy út 60, 1062, Budapest, Hungary
  • Website: https://www.terrorhaza.hu/en
  • Focus: Hungary under the Arrow Cross (Nazi) and communist regimes, in the former secret police headquarters
  • Admission: paid ($)
  • Hours: Tue–Sun 10:00–18:00 (closed Mondays and public holidays)
  • Booking: no
  • Priority: must-see
  • Visited: planned

Why Go

The building at Andrássy út 60 was the headquarters of the Arrow Cross party, and later of the communist secret police (ÁVO/ÁVH). The museum tells the story of both regimes, ending with the reconstructed prison cells in the basement. It’s very theatrical and widely called the most controversial museum in Budapest, because critics say it puts more weight on communist crimes than fascist ones. It’s a good companion to the Museum of Communism in Prague.

Highlights / Must-Sees

  • The Soviet tank in the atrium and the wall of victims’ photos
  • The basement prison cells
  • The “Hall of Tears” and the elevator ride down

Notes

  • About 10–15 minutes’ walk up Andrássy from the hotel (near Oktogon).
  • Reported price is 4,000 HUF per adult, plus 2,000 HUF for the audio guide. Tickets are sold on site only. These figures may be out of date, so check before going.
  • Plan on 1–2 hours. Room labels are mostly in Hungarian, with English info sheets in each room, so the audio guide is worth it.
  • No photos allowed inside.
