Vasarely Museum

  • Address: Szentlélek tér 6, 1033, Budapest, Hungary
  • Website: https://en.vasarely.hu/
  • Focus: Op art, through the work of Hungarian-French artist Victor Vasarely
  • Admission: paid ($)
  • Hours: Wed–Sun 10:00–18:00 (closed Mon–Tue)
  • Booking: no
  • Priority: optional
  • Visited: planned

Why Go

Victor Vasarely, born in Pécs, is called the father of op art: geometric paintings that seem to move, bulge and flicker. The museum opened in 1987 in a wing of the Baroque Zichy Palace in Óbuda. It shows the collection Vasarely gave to the city. It’s a small, very visual museum and a nice break from the history-heavy sites.

Highlights / Must-Sees

  • The large op-art works from the 1960s and 70s
  • His early graphic design and advertising work, from before his op-art period

Notes

  • Check that it’s open. The official site said it would close from 9 February to 15 September while the Museum of Fine Arts held a big Vasarely 120 retrospective (May–August 2026). It should have reopened by now.
  • The official site lists a full-price ticket at 3,800 HUF.
  • The hours come from Tripadvisor. An older listing says Tue–Sun 10:00–17:30, so check.
  • It’s in Óbuda, north on the Buda side. Take the HÉV train to Árpád híd, or a taxi (about 20 minutes). You could combine it with the Roman ruins at Aquincum.
