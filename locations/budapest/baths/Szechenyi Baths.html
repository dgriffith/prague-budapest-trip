Széchenyi Thermal Bath

  • Address: Állatkerti körút 9-11, 1146, Budapest, Hungary
  • Website: https://www.szechenyifurdo.hu
  • Style: neo-baroque
  • Price: $$$
  • Hours: Weekdays 7:00–20:00, weekends and holidays 8:00–20:00
  • Night bathing: yes
  • Booking: recommended
  • Priority: must-see
  • Visited: planned

Why Go

The big yellow neo-baroque palace in City Park is the classic Budapest bath, and one of the largest bath complexes in Europe. The outdoor pools are the image everyone knows: steam rising off hot water, with locals playing chess on floating boards. It’s touristy, but it’s worth doing once.

What to Expect

  • 18 pools in all: 15 indoor thermal and plunge pools and three big outdoor pools, plus saunas and steam rooms.
  • The outdoor pools are the main event, and are open all year. They’re at their best on a cold or misty day.
  • Lockers or a private changing cabin, both included in ticket options.
  • “Sparty” night parties on some Saturdays. It’s a party crowd, so it’s fun or awful depending on what you want.

Notes

  • Price checked online, but only 2025 figures were available: 11,900 HUF (about €30) for a weekday ticket with a locker. Weekends cost more. 2026 prices may be higher, so check the official site.
  • Hours come from a current Tripadvisor ticket listing. Ticket sales stop about an hour before closing.
  • Buy online in advance to skip the ticket queue.
  • Bring your own towel, flip-flops and swimsuit. Renting them on site is expensive.
  • In City Park, next to the Museum of Fine Arts (Szépművészeti Múzeum), Museum of Ethnography (Néprajzi Múzeum) and House of Music Hungary (Magyar Zene Háza). Pair it with a museum day: museums in the morning, a soak in the late afternoon. From the Corinthia Budapest, take the M1 metro to Széchenyi fürdő, or walk about 25 minutes up Andrássy.
